If you want to dig deeper into Sketchpad and Ivan Sutherland's work, the following provides links to online resources.

The Sketchpad Thesis

Ivan Sutherland's Sketchpad thesis, "Sketchpad: A Man-Machine Graphical Communication System" (1963), is available in a few different forms. The most authoritative version is available on MIT DSpace:

Alan Blackwell and Kerry Rodden of the University of Cambridge made a cleaned-up version: Sketchpad thesis (University of Cambridge)

There is also a technical report that predates the thesis above: Sketchpad technical report. The content is essentially the same, but the rounded fonts used in the abstract and captions are Lincoln Laboratory fonts that offer a glimpse into the history.

References

The Sketchpad thesis lists 12 papers and documents in its references. Some of them are available online.

Bill Buxton has links to several papers on his site: Bill Buxton's Lincoln Laboratory resources

Bret Victor also has links to many other papers: Bret Victor's reference collection

Of the 12 references listed in the thesis, the following have links where available:

  1. "The Lincoln TX-2 Computer," Clark et al., 1957 PDF
  2. "Notes on Graphical Input Methods," Coons, 1960.
  3. "A Graphical Input Device and Shape Description Interpretation Routines," Johnston, 1960.
  4. "Man-Computer Symbiosis," Licklider, 1960. PDF
  5. "On-Line Man-Computer Communication," Licklider and Clark, 1962. ACM Digital Library
  6. "Graphical Manipulation Techniques Using the Lincoln TX-2 Computer," Loomis, 1960.
  7. "On the Shortest Path Through a Maze," Moore, 1959.
  8. "Machine Perception of Three-Dimensional Solids," Roberts, 1963. MIT DSpace
  9. "Relaxation Methods in Engineering Science," Southwell, 1940. Book listing
  10. "TX-2 Users Handbook," Vanderburgh, 1961. PDF
  11. "Computer Utilization," Walsh and Smith, 1959.
  12. "Handbook for Variplotter Models 205S and 205T, PACE," 1959.

Ivan Sutherland Recounts the History of Sketchpad

Ivan Sutherland generally prefers not to look back on his own past work, but on one occasion in 1994, he gave a 90-minute talk recounting the history of Sketchpad and sharing personal anecdotes about its development.

Notable Writings

There are quite a few notable writings by Ivan Sutherland.

"The Ultimate Display" (1965)

"The Ultimate Display" was a proposal for his next project, a head-mounted display. It was a precursor to modern virtual reality headsets. The proposal also shows his dry sense of humor.

The Ultimate Display (PDF)

"Technology and Courage"

Another notable work is "Technology and Courage."

Technology and Courage (PDF)

It articulates the preparation and iterative processes involved in research, as well as the role courage plays in the adventure of doing research. The document evolved from a lecture given in 1982 into a published version in 1990.
